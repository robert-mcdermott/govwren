Standards alignment

People adopting GovWren are often asked how it relates to a standard: the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, ISO/IEC 42001, HIPAA, or a list of risks for AI agents. This guide answers that plainly: what GovWren gives you evidence for, what it leaves to you, and how to produce the documents an assessor asks for.

Nothing here is needed to use GovWren. The control library stands on its own, and a framework is a second way of looking at the same controls.

What GovWren is in a standard's terms#

GovWren is a system of record for a governance program. It holds the inventory, the decisions and their reasons, the controls and their evidence, and a history that cannot be quietly changed. In a standard's terms it is where documented information lives and where you show that a process was followed.

It is passive. It does not scan networks, read logs, test models, or enforce anything inside another system. So for any requirement there are three honest answers, and this guide uses them throughout:

AnswerMeans
GovWren does itThe requirement is about keeping a record, running a review, or deciding with reasons, and GovWren is where that happens
GovWren holds the evidenceSomeone else does the work, in another system or on paper, and GovWren records that it was done, by whom, and with what proof
Outside GovWrenThe requirement is about something GovWren has no part in, and no control answers it. It shows as Not mapped, never as covered

No software makes an organization compliant. GovWren does not certify anything, and an assessor or your own counsel decides whether what you hold is enough.

How alignment works#

  1. Controls are what your program does. Each has a standing, an owner, evidence, and a date it is next checked.
  2. A framework pack lists a standard's requirements and maps each to the controls that answer it.
  3. Readiness is worked out, not typed in: a requirement is covered when every control mapped to it is in place with evidence that is still good.
  4. The statement of applicability is the same picture as a spreadsheet, for a person outside the program.

Four frameworks are built in. Load one from Frameworks, or with POST /api/v1/frameworks. Loading changes no control and no record.

FrameworkRequirementsAnswered by a controlWhat the pack contains
NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.01918Codes, with summaries in GovWren's words
ISO/IEC 42001:20236453Clause and control numbers, with labels and summaries in GovWren's words. None of the standard's text: you need your own copy

The summaries are reading aids. The standard or the regulation is the authority, and where a summary and the source differ, the source is right.

NIST AI Risk Management Framework#

The framework is voluntary and organized as four functions. GovWren maps to its categories.

FunctionGovWren does itGovWren holds the evidence
GovernRoles, policies with acknowledgment, the inventory, review rules, decisions with reasons, exceptions that end, retirementTraining of reviewers, leadership review
MapIntake: purpose, people affected, data, autonomy; the tier and why; impact assessments
MeasureThe program's own measures; spot checks of automatic approvalsTesting and evaluation of a model, done by its owners, attached as evidence
ManageConditions, periodic review, incidents, changes declared and reviewed, risks with treatments and ownersMonitoring of a system in use

One category is not mapped: the one about the make-up and diversity of the workforce that governs AI. That belongs to how you staff the program, and no record in GovWren answers it.

ISO/IEC 42001#

ISO/IEC 42001 describes a management system for AI: clauses 4 to 10, and a set of controls in Annex A. Certification is granted by an accredited body after an audit. GovWren is not certified and cannot certify you. What it can do is hold most of what the audit looks at.

4 Context, 5 LeadershipScope, roles, the policy and who has acknowledged itLeadership's commitment, as approvals and minutes
6 PlanningThe risk register, treatments, impact assessments, objectives as measuresPlanning of changes to the management system itself
7 SupportPolicies and records as controlled documents; reviewer training as a controlCompetence and awarenessResources; communication plans
8 OperationIntake, tiering, review, decision, conditions
9 Performance evaluationMeasures, reports to leadership, the dossierInternal audit and management review
10 ImprovementFindings, actions, incidents, exceptions
A.2 to A.5Policies, roles, the inventory of systems, models, data, and vendors, impact assessmentHow the AI policy fits with your other policies; impacts on society as a whole
A.6 Life cycleRecords of a system from request to retirementVerification, deployment, and monitoring by the people who build or run itDesign and development process, for organizations that build AI
A.7 DataDatasets with their class, origin, terms, and quality checksData preparation

Eleven requirements are not mapped, and the table's last column says where they fall. If your organization only uses AI and does not build it, several of the life cycle controls may not apply: mark them Does not apply… with the reason, and that reason is carried into the statement of applicability.

HIPAA#

GovWren can help a covered entity or business associate show how AI tools are governed. Three points come first.

  • GovWren should hold no health information. Forms ask people to describe data, not paste it. Keep it that way.
  • The technical safeguards are performed by the systems that hold the data. Access control, audit logs, integrity, and encryption happen in the AI tool and around it. GovWren holds the evidence that they are in place, as the control Access, logging, and encryption are shown for tools that hold sensitive data.
  • This is not legal advice. Whether an arrangement needs a business associate agreement, and whether data is de-identified, are judgments for your privacy officer and counsel. GovWren records the judgment and who made it.
AreaGovWren does itGovWren holds the evidence
Risk analysis and risk managementThe inventory of every AI tool that touches health information, its tier, its risks and treatmentsThe security risk analysis itself, if it is done elsewhere
Assigned responsibility, workforceNamed owners and roles; policies and acknowledgmentTraining records
Business associatesAgreements, the AI systems each covers, and the rule that stops approval without oneThe signed agreement
EvaluationPeriodic review, declared changes, spot checks
Technical safeguardsAccess control, audit controls, integrity, authentication, transmission security
Uses and disclosures, minimum necessary, de-identificationIntake questions, privacy review, conditions on the approvalThe de-identification determination
Breach notificationIncidents, with what happened and what was decidedThe risk assessment and the notices sent

One requirement is not mapped: contingency planning, which is about backup and recovery of the systems that hold the data.

Agents#

The OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications is a list of ten risks of AI systems that act by themselves. It is not a standard to comply with, and loading it shows which of the ten your controls answer for the agents in your inventory.

RiskGovWren holds the evidence that
ASI01 Goal hijack, ASI06 Memory and context poisoningWhat the agent reads from outside is treated as untrusted, and a person checks its output
ASI02 Tool misuse, ASI03 Identity and privilege abuseThe agent has the least access its task needs, under an identity of its own
ASI04 Agentic supply chainModel versions and sources are recorded, and agreements with suppliers are on file
ASI05 Unexpected code executionCode and commands run somewhere contained
ASI08 Cascading failures, ASI10 Rogue agentsThe agent can be stopped and that has been tried; its approval is reviewed and its owner confirms its record
ASI09 Exploiting people's trustA person checks output before it is used, and users are told the tool's limits

All of this is evidence of work done in and around the agent. GovWren does not watch an agent run, and cannot stop one. One risk is not mapped: ASI07, insecure communication between agents, which is settled in how the agents are built.

The agent profile and the agent assessment are where the facts behind these controls are recorded.

Produce the statement of applicability#

An assessor usually asks for one document that lists every requirement, says whether it applies, why not if it does not, and what answers it.

  1. Open Frameworks, then the framework.
  2. For each requirement that does not apply to you, choose Does not apply… and give the reason.
  3. Choose Statement of applicability. The spreadsheet has one row for each requirement: its code, its title, whether it applies, the justification, how it stands, and the controls that answer it with where each stands.

The same list comes from GET /api/v1/statement-of-applicability?framework=iso-iec-42001, as CSV or, with format=json, as JSON. Without framework, the list is the control library instead, which needs no framework loaded.

For the evidence behind the rows, make a dossier of a record, or open a control to see what is attached and when it was last checked.

Another standard#

A framework is a data file. If you answer to a standard that is not built in, write a pack for it: its requirements by code, and which controls answer each. Frameworks in the Assurance guide describes the format. Use the publisher's codes and your own words; do not copy text you have no right to distribute.

What stays your job#

  • Deciding which standards you answer to, and what is in scope.
  • Doing the technical work the records describe.
  • Keeping evidence current. GovWren tells you when it has expired; it cannot renew it.
  • Reading the standard. A pack is a map of it, not a replacement.
